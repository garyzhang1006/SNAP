\subsection{Common step rule}
\label{app:schedule}
For each of the 125 configurations, we selected the largest checkpoint step released for all three runs. The supplement described in the analysis record omits the fetch stage's complete configuration table. DataDecide stops the two auxiliary runs below 1B at 25 percent of the 1B compute budget and trains three complete runs only at 1B \citep{magnusson2025}. The per-instance release contains auxiliary runs from 150M upward, so the replicated sizes begin at 150M rather than 4M.

An index of the 25 released recipe archives reproduces the selected step recorded for all 375 runs. At 530M the selected step averages 86.7 percent of the default run's final step, with configuration values from 38.9 to 88.7 percent. At 750M it averages 41.5 percent, with values from 17.7 to 44.0 percent. In 92 configurations the three runs have different maximum steps. In 26 of them, one at 530M and 25 at 750M, the largest maximum step exceeds the smallest by more than a factor of 1.5. Only 33 configurations share a final step, and 24 of those sit at 1B.

Table~\ref{tab:truncation} recomputes both estimates on subsets defined by the index. The full sample gives margin inflation of 1.244 with interval 1.143 to 1.338 and accuracy inflation of 1.078 with interval 0.993 to 1.157. Excluding the 26 severely truncated configurations gives 1.276 with interval 1.114 to 1.420 and 1.120 for accuracy, and neither wild interval includes one. The 92 configurations with different final steps give 1.330 with interval 1.024 to 1.583 and 1.070 with interval 0.956 to 1.171. The 33 configurations with a shared final step give intervals too wide to separate inflation from one, and 18 of their 24 recipes contribute only a 1B configuration. A configuration's influence, $(T_c-\widehat\Lam^2U_c)/\sum_{c'}U_{c'}$, has Spearman correlation $-0.036$ for margins and $0.001$ for accuracy with the selected step's share of the default final step. Permuting that share within each size over 4,999 draws gives $p=0.314$ and $p=0.990$. We chose these subsets after seeing the full-sample estimates, so they describe sensitivity rather than testing a hypothesis. Table~\ref{tab:original7} gives estimates by size as a further check.

\subsection{Decision checks and deviations}
The planning document lists nine checks with proposed responses, and its claimed date of September 5, 2026 lacks an independent timestamp, so we treat it as a reported plan and its checks as exploratory assessments.

A batch offset can enter the same cross-trait products as run-level variation, and only the auxiliary-run contrast removes it. Check G8 passes on point estimates, with an auxiliary-run margin of 1.256 against 1.122, although the intervals cross the decision boundaries and the full-set estimate stays uncorrected for this exposure.

Under the default gain simulator, $\sigma_g=0.05$ produces approximately 61 percent of the observed margin excess. An empirically matched simulator produces approximately eight percent at the same gain scale and six percent at the estimated scale of 0.042. These results describe sensitivity to simulator assumptions and aren't causal bounds on the contribution of gain. The accuracy result offers no independent protection because multiplying a margin by a positive factor leaves its sign unchanged. Table~\ref{tab:original5} records every planning check beside its recorded outcome. Those outcomes include the 125 parsed cells at G0, accuracy power at $\bar r_E=0.115$ for G1, the ratio threshold of 1.4 for G2, and an offset of at most 0.002 for G4.

\subsection{Reported planning predictions}
The planning document specifies eleven predictions with rejection conditions, and Table~\ref{tab:original6} lists each criterion with its outcome without treating the document as verified preregistration. The first prediction requires $\Lambda_M>1.349$ and $\Lambda_A>1.40$ with lower confidence limits above one, and the second requires $\Lambda_A\geq0.8\Lambda_M$. Predictions three and four bound the accuracy excess retained after gain adjustment, the N5 share of the margin excess, and the N6 share of the full-set excess. Predictions five to seven concern underprediction by independence across size bands, transport to PolyPythias, and an external seed mechanism with a bootstrap lower limit above one. Prediction eight requires at least eight traits with information ratios between 1.4 and 3.0, and prediction nine a cross-format diagnostic above 1.2. Prediction ten requires a Frobenius distance above 0.5, and prediction eleven more than ten percent of same-size configuration pairs with $P_{\rm flip}>0.05$.
\subsection{Excluded populations}
MultiBERTs provides 25 seeds for one configuration \citep{sellam2022}, whereas the 100 BERT runs of \citet{mccoy2020} vary fine-tuning from one pretrained checkpoint. Pythia's size ladder lacks seed replicates \citep{biderman2023}. The OLMo-2 checkpoints considered here lack replicates at matched steps \citep{teamolmo2025}. The signal-and-noise panel lacks per-item outputs, which limits its use to the external check. We exclude these populations because they lack the matched replicates or item outputs required here.

\subsection{Relation to prior measurement work}
\citet{reimers2017}, \citet{madaan2024}, and \citet{hagmann2023} measure seed variance with scalar variance components. Our target adds covariance between benchmark-specific run deviations. \citet{jordan2024} compares shifted versions of one benchmark, and \citet{bui2025} and \citet{hochlehnert2025} vary fine-tuning and decoding seeds, which differ from pretraining-run variation. \citet[Section 4.3]{dodge2020} compare four GLUE task rankings across seeds without a covariance matrix. The Spearman matrix of \citet[Figure 3]{zhou2020} averages checkpoints across seeds, mixing checkpoint progress with seed deviations. \citet{dufour2026}, \citet{miller2024}, and \citet{zhao2026} address training versus sampling variation, item-level uncertainty for one model, and per-task tests without cross-task covariance. \citet{haupt2026} estimate per-item variation on PolyPythias, which differs from the covariance of benchmark run effects.

\citet{cheverud2001}, \citet{nyholt2004}, and \citet{li2005} count effective independent tests to adjust significance thresholds, whereas we convert a noise covariance into an effective benchmark count. \citet{searle1992} explain negative moment estimates when the true component lies near zero. \citet{schaeffer2023} show how discontinuous metrics change apparent trends, and accuracy thresholds our margin at zero, so we report both scales, although low seed signal relative to item noise limits accuracy here. \citet{dehghani2021}, \citet{demsar2006}, \citet{dror2017}, \citet{card2020}, \citet{gorman2019}, and \citet{burnell2023} address benchmark selection, inference, power, split dependence, and per-instance reporting. \citet{henderson2018} and \citet{agarwal2021} resample runs within tasks, whereas we must preserve paired run effects across benchmarks. \citet{hardy2026}, \citet{kohli2026}, \citet{messing2026}, and \citet{sha2026} measure dependence across models, judges, or prompts, whereas we measure paired deviations across training runs.

The cross-half identity follows \citet{spearman1904}, and \citet{cronbach1951} motivates the 50 re-splits in N3, which change the margin estimate by a reported standard deviation of 0.002. The \citet{brown1910} and \citet{spearman1910} formula shares the form $1+(k-1)\rho$ with $\Lambda^2$, but its correlation concerns parallel items, so the reliability means in Table~\ref{tab:original3} don't estimate our inflation. \citet{campbell1959} cross traits with methods, whereas no benchmark here appears in multiple formats, so content and scoring format can't be separated. \citet{brennan2001} provides the variance-component framework for these distinctions. \citet{hofmann2025}, who share seven authors with \citet{heineman2025}, and \citet{kipnis2024} describe ability structures across models rather than within-configuration noise covariance. \citet{hedges2010} handle dependent effect sizes without a complete covariance matrix, whereas we estimate that covariance.

On an accuracy-like band the log interval misses in 0.151 of replicates against 0.017 for the plain one, so it buys definedness by paying coverage. \paragraph{Permutation comparison}
N2 was proposed as an estimator-bias check rather than the primary inferential procedure. In the reported 500 permutations, none reaches the observed margin estimate, and 0.028 reach the accuracy estimate. The observed configurations are held fixed under this reassignment, while trait-specific run labels are permuted independently. The primary bootstrap instead targets variation across recipe clusters, so the two procedures use different randomisation and sampling assumptions. We report both accuracy results because the disagreement depends on their different randomisation and sampling assumptions.

\paragraph{Associations with scoring format}
The cross-format diagnostic retains the diagonal sum and only those off-diagonal terms whose traits have different formats. It has a null reference of one when those retained covariances vanish, but the masked sum needn't represent the variance of a realisable benchmark average. Because each benchmark has only one format, the comparison can't identify a causal effect of format.

The separate calculation reproduces the full estimate to four decimal places before retaining 54 of the 90 ordered off-diagonal pairs. It gives 0.921 with interval 0.801 to 1.035 for margins, and 0.965 with interval 0.848 to 1.063 for accuracy, both below the proposed 1.2 threshold. The margin result falls below the 1.153 expected from uniformly distributed covariance contributions under the comparison used here.

BoolQ is the only yes/no benchmark, so all its off-diagonal pairs cross a format boundary. Its margin seed variance is $1.43\times10^{-3}$, compared with $4.34\times10^{-4}$ for the next-largest trait. Excluding BoolQ gives a cross-format margin diagnostic of 1.006 with interval 0.972 to 1.039, compared with an unrestricted nine-trait estimate of 1.786 with interval 1.689 to 1.877.

Among the nine traits with defined margin correlations, excluding WinoGrande, the mean correlation is 0.624 across sixteen within-format pairs and 0.007 across twenty cross-format pairs. The three cloze traits give margin inflation of 1.225 with interval 1.064 to 1.367. Excluding ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge from the multiple-choice block leaves inflation of 1.479 with interval 1.416 to 1.541. These associations show concentration within the larger format groups, while benchmark content and format remain confounded. A rank-one model with trait-specific loadings can also produce uneven contributions across those groups.

\paragraph{Scoring-scale interventions}
\label{app:scale}
We transformed the saved margins item by item and recomputed each estimate with the items, runs, split, and interval routines held fixed. For benchmark $j$, $s_j$ is the pooled standard deviation of its per-item margins, which ranges from 0.087 for WinoGrande to 0.468 for CommonsenseQA. Table~\ref{tab:scale} reports the resulting estimates, and a tenfold rescaling of every margin leaves the estimate unchanged to nine decimal places, which checks the construction.

Division by $s_j$ changes only the benchmark weights and lowers margin inflation from 1.244 to 1.182, and the three bounded scores lower it further to between 1.120 and 1.141. The sign score gives 1.079, which differs from the accuracy estimate of 1.078 by 0.0007 for reasons we haven't traced. Across these scores the off-diagonal sum falls from 0.547 to 0.164 times the trace, so bounded margins from the same runs reproduce between 62 and 75 percent of the gap between margin and accuracy inflation. These transformations change the scoring rule and leave the trained models untouched, so they don't show how training produces large margins, and the intervals treat each $s_j$ as fixed.

\paragraph{Performance correlation as a noise proxy}
The reported Frobenius distances between $R_E$ and $R_P$ are 5.59 for margins and 6.49 for accuracy. Negative estimated variances leave eighteen margin and 34 accuracy ordered off-diagonal correlations undefined. The implementation fills these entries with zero before differencing, including undefined diagonal entries compared with unit diagonals in $R_P$. Those diagonal substitutions contribute 1.0 and 2.0 to the squared distances.

Repairing only those diagonals gives distances of 5.499 and 6.339. Restricting the comparison to jointly defined entries gives 4.374 and 4.593, which still exceed the planned threshold of 0.5. The threshold alone offers limited discrimination between different ten-trait matrices, so the entrywise comparisons provide a more useful description.

The off-diagonal association between the matrices is 0.756 for margins and 0.345 for accuracy. Mean defined $R_E$ entries are 0.281 and 0.273, compared with all-pair $R_P$ means of 0.750 and 0.754. On matching pairs, the $R_P$ means are 0.743 and 0.769. The all-pair comparison therefore differs in scale by factors of approximately 2.7 and 2.8. The unadjusted proxy predicts a mean margin aggregate standard deviation of 0.0072, compared with the reported 0.0057.

An additional exploratory baseline, P2R, rescales $R_P$ off-diagonals by the training-fold ratio of mean noise correlation to mean performance correlation. Its mean factors are 0.366 for margins and 0.370 for accuracy, compared with whole-population ratios of 0.375 and 0.362. On the same five recipe folds, its squared prediction errors are 0.0131 and 0.0342. It improves on P2 but remains 2.3 times worse than P1 on margins and worse than independence on accuracy. Its direction relative to independence holds on margins in all 1,000 random fold partitions and on accuracy in 987 of them, but the recipe-bootstrap intervals below include zero on both scales. This rescaling also requires replicated runs to estimate its factor.

\paragraph{Interval construction and cluster sensitivity}
A percentile bootstrap over 125 configurations gives intervals of 1.069 to 1.426 for margins and 0.976 to 1.182 for accuracy. The margin interval excludes one, and the accuracy interval includes one. Comparing squared linearised standard errors, using 0.045 against 0.094 on margins and 0.038 against 0.052 on accuracy, gives ratios of 0.23 and 0.53. The implementation separately reports numerator-based design effects \citep{kish1965} of 2.06 and 1.26 from $1+4\rho_{\rm ICC}$, compared with the planned 1.60.

These ratios describe different quantities and aren't contradictory estimates of one design effect, because the numerator calculation uses $T_c$, while linearisation uses $T_c-\widehat\Lambda^2U_c$. The interval procedures also change resampling unit, studentisation, and denominator treatment together. We consequently don't infer a single intraclass correlation from their relative widths. The bootstrap-t addresses concerns associated with few-cluster approximations, as discussed by \citet{bell2002}, but it doesn't guarantee coverage at 25 clusters.

\paragraph{Observed gaps and predicted rank reversals}
With accuracy noise $\sigma_{\rm agg}=0.00514$, the Gaussian calculation gives $P_{\rm flip}>0.05$ for a true gap below 0.01196, and 455 of the $5\binom{25}{2}=1{,}500$ observed same-size pairs fall below it. Observed gaps are noisy, so this count isn't a lower bound on the fraction of true gaps below the threshold and doesn't measure realised reversal frequency.

We also simulated a practitioner's correction under three-run noise, over 4,000 replicates that build the shipped design and estimate the ratio from it. Each replicate then tests a fresh comparison under an uncorrected rule, a plug-in rule scaled by the estimate, and an oracle rule with the true value. At a true margin gap of zero the rules declare in 0.113, 0.051 and 0.050 of replicates against a nominal 0.05, and three runs per model leave those at 0.115, 0.053 and 0.051. The plug-in rule lands within 0.001 of the oracle despite the estimate's noise, and an accuracy-like population gives 0.067, 0.052 and 0.047.

Sweeps to ten, 80 and 360 runs per model show that extra runs buy nothing against the size error, since both standard errors fall with the square root of the run count. Across one to ten runs the uncorrected rule declares at a zero margin gap in 0.112 to 0.122 of replicates against 0.047 to 0.056 corrected, and accuracy gives 0.061 to 0.078 against 0.047 to 0.063. Runs buy power instead, from 0.100 of replicates at one run to 0.614 at ten for a margin gap of one aggregate standard deviation, or 0.111 to 0.615 on accuracy. Four fifths arrives only at 16 runs for a margin gap of one aggregate standard deviation, against three shipped, and at 20 on accuracy, where even the oracle misses it at 16 by 0.002.

Both sweeps estimate the correction on the source population, so we also swept its transfer to targets whose true ratio differs, with 8,000 replicates per target. A correction from the shipped margin design, whose true ratio is 1.245, holds nominal size at 0.053 only at a target of 1.245. Below that it turns conservative, at 0.015 of replicates for a target of 1.00 and 0.028 at 1.10, and pays for that in power. Above it the size error returns, at 0.080 for a target of 1.40 and 0.101 at 1.50 against 0.159 and 0.188 uncorrected, while a matched rule re-estimated on each target holds between 0.048 and 0.053. The accuracy design as source, with true ratio 1.078, gives the same shape, holding size at 0.056 for a target of 1.10 and 0.168 at 1.50. The transferred rule holds between 0.04 and 0.06 only within about 0.05 of the source ratio, so readers should measure the ratio on their own battery.

The four cells with dispersion 0.044, close to the observed null dispersions of 0.046 and 0.048, cover 0.950, 0.935, 0.975, and 0.960. This limited simulation does not validate all departures present in the study.

A second simulation with 2,000 replicates per population and 4,999 draws scores four interval procedures on the same simulated moments. Table~\ref{tab:coverage} lists the populations, each with 125 configurations in 25 recipe clusters, three runs, and ten benchmarks. The wild bootstrap-$t$ covers between 0.928 and 0.954, and the cluster $t(24)$ tracks it within 0.02 but falls to 0.911 with one recipe at six times the variance. The configuration percentile bootstrap falls to 0.872 under recipe-shared run effects, and the recipe percentile bootstrap covers between 0.857 and 0.940 with undefined draws in the accuracy-like noise cells. Cross-half error correlation of 0.1 biases the estimate by $-0.014$ and $-0.016$. Its margin-like wild interval misses above in 0.066 of replicates against a nominal 0.025, so the shortfall falls on one side, and the accuracy-like population with mean correlation 0.018 carries a bias of $-0.007$.

We later added a centred cluster test-inversion interval, whose accepted set can be unbounded and is bounded in 23,999 of the same 24,000 simulated datasets. It covers between 0.932 and 0.957 and holds 0.955 with one recipe at six times the variance, where the wild interval gives 0.930. A restricted variant without centring covers between 0.946 and 0.970, above 0.95 in eleven of the twelve populations. A repeat of all twelve populations at 10,000 replicates puts the centred inversion between 0.935 and 0.956 and the wild interval between 0.933 and 0.953.

Since the Monte Carlo standard error of 0.005 is the size of the shortfalls in Table~\ref{tab:coverage}, we repeated every cell at 10,000 replicates with new seeds, and no cell moves by more than 0.011. In the four cells that matter most, the wild interval covers 0.933 with cross-half correlation 0.1, 0.935 with one dominant recipe, 0.940 in the margin-like cell, and 0.945 in the accuracy-like cell. A third seed moves the margin-like cell to 0.951 before it settles at 0.946 over 60,000 pooled replicates, while the other three repeat within 0.004. The centred inversion covers 0.935, 0.956, 0.942, and 0.947 in the same four cells, so it keeps its advantage of 0.021 under one dominant recipe while matching the wild interval elsewhere. A further sweep with a third seed puts the wild interval between 0.929 and 0.950 across all twelve, so the shortfall belongs to the construction rather than to one set of draws.

We then applied all eight constructions to the data itself with 9,999 draws over twenty bootstrap seeds. On the full battery the margin interval runs from 1.141 to 1.339 under Rademacher weights, 1.130 to 1.349 under residual scaling, and 1.095 to 1.323 under the centred inversion. All eight exclude one on margins and include one on accuracy. Removal of BoolQ leaves both scales above one under all eight, where the widest margin interval is the restricted inversion at 1.391 to 1.840. The same replicates scored at one in ten, one in twenty and one in a hundred give 0.896, 0.951 and 0.990 on an independent population, so the interval tracks the level it claims. A band-shared quarter breaks all three levels together, at 0.743, 0.828 and 0.928.

We compared six further constructions on the same twelve populations. A wild bootstrap-$t$ whose recipe residual is divided by $1-h_g$, with $h_g$ the recipe's share of the denominator sum, covers between 0.943 and 0.958 at 4,000 replicates against 0.932 to 0.951 for the shipped version. At 10,000 replicates it improves every cell by between 0.007 and 0.014, reaches 0.95 in ten cells where the shipped version reaches it in one, and runs 4 to 13 percent wider at the median. We picked it after seeing the first four cells, so the eight that followed confirm it on the same generator family rather than test it independently, and we report the shipped interval throughout. On the observed data it gives \ci{1.130}{1.349} for margins and \ci{0.987}{1.163} for accuracy, which moves no conclusion but erases the 0.011 of separation from the planned threshold that we report below.

Prepivoting measures calibration inside the resampling model that the interval already assumes, so a violation the bootstrap shares with the estimator stays invisible to it. Beran prepivoting, a second wild bootstrap of 1,999 draws inside each of 1,999 outer resamples, calibrates the full-battery margin interval to \ci{1.151}{1.328}, which runs 0.018 narrower than the shipped \ci{1.143}{1.338} and still excludes one. Accuracy calibrates to \ci{0.994}{1.161} and still includes one, and the BoolQ-removed margin interval calibrates to \ci{1.689}{1.878}. In the four simulated cells that carry the shortfall it gives 0.924, 0.933, 0.931 and 0.944 against 0.938, 0.931, 0.936 and 0.953 for the shipped interval, so it repairs none of the shortfall.

The estimator assumes that item noise in one half does not correlate with item noise in the other, so we swept the cross-half noise correlation from $-0.1$ to 0.5 with 1,000 replicates at each value. The mean margin estimate stays between 0.997 and 1.001 and no margin replicate reaches the observed 1.244, because such noise pulls a real inflation toward one. On accuracy the null dispersion is wide enough that 0.19 of replicates reach the observed 1.078 with no correlation at all.

A scalar effect shared across benchmarks and repeated across halves is a different matter, because the numerator absorbs all of it and the denominator only a tenth. We swept its share of item-noise variance with 2,000 replicates at each value, and a share near 0.124 reproduces the observed 1.244. At a share of 0.05 only 0.007 of replicates reach the observed value, so small amounts of this contamination do not explain the margin result. Accuracy is far more fragile, because the same construction reaches the observed 1.078 at a share near 0.009. We have not measured this component in the released scores, and a regression diagnostic cannot separate it from a seed covariance that follows the item-noise scales, so that check has no power.

A permutation of run deviations across the items of one group, separately for every run, breaks only the tie between particular items and particular runs. Over 2,000 permutations of the 37,682 items in their 66 groups, 0.087 of the permutations reach or exceed the observed margin 1.244, and 0.352 reach or exceed the observed accuracy. The item-level channel therefore accounts for about 0.001 of the margin estimate and 0.002 of the accuracy estimate, against excesses over one of 0.244 and 0.078. Re-drawing the item half split 200 times gives a margin range of 1.242 to 1.254 and an accuracy median of 1.083 with a range of 1.059 to 1.103. The shipped 1.244 and 1.078 are therefore not a favourable draw. The real re-split standard deviations of 0.0018 and 0.0083 exceed the permuted 0.0009 and 0.0053, so item-level structure does survive and widens the spread without moving the centre. An effect that every item of a benchmark carries survives this permutation untouched, so only the sub-benchmark form of the violation is bounded here.

These scenarios are fitted to the same data that the intervals describe, so they are not independent confirmation.

\paragraph{Availability of a separate competence measurement}
The desired gain control requires per-run loss on held-out text separate from the benchmark items. The DataDecide fields inspected in the source analysis don't supply it. The PolyPythias evaluation files considered there cover three sizes at early and final checkpoints, but contain accuracies, likelihood differences, and LAMBADA perplexity on the same benchmark items. That perplexity doesn't provide the required independent measurement scale.

The Mistral release of \citet{karamcheti2021} provides five GPT-2 Small and five GPT-2 Medium runs with about 600 checkpoints each, listed as 610 in the release summary, including WikiText-103 and OpenWebText validation perplexity. The source search didn't find the corresponding per-run benchmark battery. Without paired held-out loss and benchmark scores from the same runs, we can't use that release to identify the gain contribution.

\paragraph{Competence proxy audit and calibration}
\label{app:proxy}
We audited the shipped competence adjustment and two repairs on the 375 reduced runs, then applied each adjustment to simulated populations with known seed covariance. The audit reproduces the shipped after-adjustment estimates of 1.244 and 1.076. The variance floor binds for WinoGrande on margins and for PIQA and WinoGrande on accuracy. WinoGrande then supplies more than 99.99 percent of the squared standardised inputs to the margin proxy, so the shipped margin adjustment in effect regresses every other benchmark on WinoGrande. Removal of WinoGrande from the proxy gives 0.890 with interval 0.805 to 0.968, while removal of any other benchmark leaves margin inflation between 1.244 and 1.245.

The repair we fixed before seeing its output standardises by seed variances shrunk halfway toward their median positive value, cross-fitted over recipe folds. It gives margin inflation of 0.731 with interval 0.673 to 0.784, and accuracy inflation of 1.004 with interval 0.925 to 1.075.

For this revision we fitted four proxy weightings out of fold. A split-half check shows that the shipped proxy carries no measurable signal about the run it scores, since its half-A and half-B scores correlate at $-0.086$ on margins and $-0.072$ on accuracy. A one-factor proxy with regression-score weights reaches 0.965 and 0.738 on the same check.

Cross-fitted margin inflation is 1.245 under the shipped weighting, 0.731 after shrinkage, 0.973 for the factor proxy, and 0.889 when the clipped benchmarks are dropped. A 999-draw recipe bootstrap that refits the whole pipeline gives 0.935 to 1.018 for the factor proxy on margins and 0.970 to 1.104 on accuracy. The adjusted margin estimate therefore stops separating from independence. Under simulated independent seed deviations all four variants return a mean between 0.998 and 1.003, so none of them manufactures covariance where none exists. None of this identifies mediation, because every one of these proxies averages the same seed deviations that it adjusts and so removes shared covariance by construction. With the observed seed covariance and no competence variable at all, the four weightings return margin means of 1.097, 0.745, 0.980, and 1.029. The observed cross-fitted values sit inside the central 90 percent of those simulations for the shipped, shrunk, and factor weightings on both scales, so none carries evidence of a mechanism beyond covariance removal. Those simulations refit the cross-fitted pipeline in every replicate, so their percentiles describe a different estimator from the in-sample matched means in Table~\ref{tab:proxy}. The clipped-drop weighting is the one departure, since its observed 0.889 on margins and 0.994 on accuracy fall below simulated fifth percentiles of 0.908 and 1.003. Under a common correlation of 0.178 the factor weighting returns 0.852 on margins, so these proxies can overshoot under equicorrelation.

Table~\ref{tab:proxy} shows the same pattern for the in-sample adjustments. With the observed seed covariance the repaired margin adjustments return 0.745 to 0.889, each within 0.015 of the observed 0.731 to 0.890. On accuracy they lower the matched simulated mean from 1.116 to between 1.049 and 1.079, and the observed values of 1.004 to 1.019 lie inside the central 90 percent of those simulations. A competence adjustment therefore needs a measurement taken outside the benchmark scores, such as held-out loss on the same checkpoints.

We then checked whether those checkpoints are reachable. An index built from the model repositories matched 120 of the 375 runs to a training branch whose released steps overlap the run's evaluation steps, with a smallest overlap of 0.042. Each of those 120 runs has a released checkpoint at its selected step. We had built the remaining names from evaluation tables that spell 17 of the 25 recipes differently. Because the Hub answers a missing repository with HTTP 401 rather than 404, we read the refusals as gating. Under the released naming all 125 repositories resolve, and every one of the 375 runs reaches a public commit with config, tokenizer and weights. Among the 120 matched runs the branch's final step disagrees with the run's final evaluation step in 21 cases, a further obstacle to scoring loss at the step the scores describe. The measurement is therefore open to anyone with the compute, and the obstacle we reported earlier was our own naming rather than access control.

\paragraph{Transport to PolyPythias}\label{app:transport}
The planned transport analysis scores nine runs per configuration on a nested 4,755-item battery. For three sizes, the source pipeline gives margin inflation of 1.40574 compared with 1.24395 for DataDecide, an excess ratio of 1.66323. On excess inflation, the DataDecide prediction falls approximately 40 percent below PolyPythias, while the raw inflation difference is approximately twelve percent and falls inside the proposed twenty-percent band. The planning document names aggregate standard deviation without resolving this scale choice, so the excess-based failure assessment is post hoc.

An exploratory extension adds the 14M and 31M configurations with nine runs each at step 143000. Within that rescore, margin inflation changes from 1.41372 at three sizes to 1.26199 at five sizes, and the excess ratio from 1.69592 to 1.07393. Accuracy changes from 2.28589 to 1.71085 against DataDecide's 1.07837, and only the three-size point estimate exceeds the stated detection threshold of approximately 2.05. The configuration-bootstrap interval for five-size accuracy runs from 0.869 to 2.428, and at three sizes its lower endpoint is undefined, with an upper endpoint of 2.971. \section{Implementation details}
\label{app:implementation}

\subsection{Data streaming}
The implementation streams 122.9 GB of gzipped JSONL tarballs from the Hugging Face release. It retains the item identifier, correct-answer margin, and accuracy bit for the ten traits at the common checkpoint. Each run contains 37,682 items and approximately 30 MB of compressed output. Reduction of 375 runs produces approximately 48 MB of NumPy archives, with one array per run.

A frozen master seed generates a balanced permutation of item order within each trait. The split uses that permutation rather than a hash of item identifiers, and all runs within an arm reuse the same trait-specific assignment. The PolyPythias subsample therefore has its own split, rather than an item-hash split shared across arms. Regenerating the split requires the recorded item ordering and randomisation specification, without downloading the full tarballs.

\subsection{Interval calculation}
Derived quantities throughout the paper are computed from unrounded estimates, so recomputation from the printed three-decimal values can differ in the final digit.

We state the source calculation in terms of the squared ratio,
\[
\widehat\theta=\frac{\sum_cT_c}{\sum_cU_c},\qquad
\widehat\Lambda=\sqrt{\widehat\theta}. \]
For each recipe $g$ among $G=25$, the linearised contribution and standard error are
\begin{equation}
\psi_g=\frac{\sum_{c\in g}(T_c-\widehat\theta U_c)}{\sum_cU_c},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}=\left(\sum_{g=1}^{G}\psi_g^2\right)^{1/2}. \label{eq:bootstrap}
\end{equation}
This standard error belongs to the squared-ratio scale used for studentisation. A reported standard error on the $\Lambda$ scale instead requires the corresponding delta-method conversion.

For draw $b$, independent recipe signs satisfy $v_{bg}\in\{-1,+1\}$ with equal probabilities, every configuration takes the sign assigned to its recipe, and the pseudo-numerator becomes \[ T_c^*=\widehat\theta U_c+v_{bg(c)}(T_c-\widehat\theta U_c). \]
The denominator remains fixed at the observed $\sum_cU_c$, and we recompute $\widehat\theta_b^*=\sum_cT_c^*/\sum_cU_c$ and the bootstrap standard error from the residuals $T_c^*-\widehat\theta_b^*U_c$. The bootstrap statistic is $t_b^*=(\widehat\theta_b^*-\widehat\theta)/\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}_b^*$, and the source calculation uses 4,999 draws with bootstrap seed zero. Every estimate and simulation in this paper ran on Python 3.12.13 with NumPy 2.0.2 under Linux, and the draws come from NumPy's default generator, whose stream is tied to that version.

Writing $q_\alpha$ for the empirical quantile of these statistics, the interval on $\theta$ is
\[
\left[\widehat\theta-q_{0.975}\widehat{\mathrm{SE}},
\widehat\theta-q_{0.025}\widehat{\mathrm{SE}}\right]. \]
Square-root transformation gives endpoints on the inflation scale when both endpoints are nonnegative. Monotonicity preserves the ordering of available endpoints, but it doesn't establish the original interval's coverage.

A draw with zero bootstrap standard error has no finite studentised statistic and is discarded. The routine requires at least $\max(100,0.9B)$ surviving draws before reporting an interval. A negative lower squared-ratio endpoint is recorded as missing rather than truncated, which explains the absent 1B margin lower endpoint in Table~\ref{tab:original7}. The configuration percentile bootstrap instead resamples all 125 configurations with replacement and recomputes both sums, while ignoring recipe membership.

The planning document specified wild cluster bootstrap-t inference with $t(16)$ critical values for seventeen estimation recipes. The realised analysis uses empirical bootstrap quantiles over all 25 recipes, following the failure to implement the planned split. The analysis record states that a $t(G-1)$ variant accompanies the bootstrap implementation. These alternatives differ in more than a change of cluster count.

\subsection{Practical considerations}
Within-configuration centring removes the shared performance mean before forming cross-half products. With only three runs, raw products would include a large contribution from mean competence rather than measuring covariance of deviations. The contrast representation in Appendix~\ref{app:centring} enforces that centring at the estimator input.

The permutation procedure reassigns run labels independently within each trait. Applying one joint permutation to all traits preserves cross-trait pairing and therefore doesn't generate this independence null. Identity permutations remain valid members of the randomisation distribution and shouldn't be excluded. For three runs, independent identities across all $K$ traits have probability $6^{-K}$ within one configuration.

Gzipped tarball members require sequential reading from their starts, and the reduction therefore extracts the required fields on one pass, since an omitted field requires another complete stream. The default-versus-auxiliary step mismatch at 530M and 750M motivates the auxiliary-two-versus-auxiliary-three contrast, with checkpoint agreement checked separately for both auxiliary runs.

The pipeline lists the 375 checkpoint directories once, applies exponential backoff to rate limits, and caches the listing. Before estimation, it regenerates the common-step table from that cache and compares it with the initial version. This check can detect changes in the recorded step selection, but a cached listing alone can't detect new remote changes without a refreshed comparison. Table~\ref{tab:original15} carries the planned compute budget for both arms.

\subsection{Release provenance}
The null checks examine mechanisms that can affect covariance estimates, but they don't independently establish the provenance of the three released runs. Differences in tokeniser builds, harness revisions, or other training conditions could contribute to the default-versus-auxiliary contrast if those conditions weren't held fixed. The auxiliary-run contrast also relies on documentation that the relevant settings match between its two runs.

The step table and disagreement checks expose some inconsistencies without replacing a release-side audit. Attribution of the observed contrasts to the documented seed changes therefore depends on the release documentation. This limitation applies independently of the estimator's behaviour on simulated or permuted data.

\subsection{Sensitivity to GPU architecture}
The primary Arm 2 scores use one card type. Relative to a margin standard deviation of 0.391, the mean difference has ratio $2.6\times10^{-3}$.

Eighteen of 4,755 accuracy bits change, or 0.38 percent, with a median absolute margin of $1.3\times10^{-3}$, and battery accuracy changes from 0.430074 to 0.430494, a difference of $4.2\times10^{-4}$. This single comparison shows sensitivity at that scale, but it doesn't establish a universal portability threshold for fp16 kernels.

Arm 1 uses released per-item outputs without recomputing forward passes, so this study doesn't add the same hardware variation to those scores. Shared hardware across Arm 2's 27 runs reduces one source of variation but doesn't independently validate accuracy across architectures.

\subsection{Centring through run contrasts}
\label{app:centring}
Raw cross-half products contain squared configuration means, and the analysis record reports that these can inflate the ratio by roughly three orders of magnitude. The described implementation projects scores onto an orthonormal basis of mean-zero run contrasts. Each basis row annihilates the all-ones vector, making raw and centred inputs equivalent under projection. Runtime checks test row sums and invariance of $\sum_c T_c$ under a shared configuration shift on both halves, addressing centring errors without testing measurement or exchangeability assumptions.
