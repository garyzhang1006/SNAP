045   A practitioner averaging K benchmarks relies on their seed-induced deviations being mutually inde-
046   pendent, giving the aggregate seed variance (1/K 2 ) tr ΣE , with ΣE the cross-benchmark covariance

049   benchmarks act like Keff = K/Λ2 independent ones however many a practitioner adds. So the noise
050
      floor rises off zero. Bouthillier et al. (2021) derived this algebra for one pipeline on one task, but

      battery tacitly sets r̄E = 0 without evidence, which we call P0.

      Bouthillier et al. (2021) derives an aggregate’s variance under correlated trainings, their Eq. 7,
418
      algebraically our Keff = K/(1 + (K − 1)ρ), and estimates that average correlation ρ on five case
419
      studies, where their ρ is between repeated trainings of one pipeline on one task, ours instead between
420   benchmarks within one run. More randomisation cannot remove the cross-benchmark term. New here
421   is the object, a K × K cross-benchmark matrix where they have a scalar. That matrix is measured
422   free of item-sampling error, up to passage leakage. Heineman et al. (2025) define noise as the relative
423   standard deviation that is computed across a run’s final n checkpoints and show that high-signal-to-
424   noise subsets of MMLU and AutoBencher beat the full set on aggregate SNR, a gap they hypothesise
425   comes from subtask quality and labelling error (Gema et al., 2025), and rank subtasks by their own
426   SNR without ever estimating the covariance between subtasks’ noise terms.
427   Seed variance is reported benchmark by benchmark, rarely as a covariance across them, whether the
428   training (Reimers & Gurevych, 2017; Madaan et al., 2024; Hagmann et al., 2023), the fine-tuning

430   test-set variance in standard trainings to per-example noise rather than distribution-level differences.

      A.1    W HAT SNAP NAMES
738
739   SNAP abbreviates Seed Noise Across Phenotypes. Each word carries a piece of the design.
740
741
      Seed. Two runs of one DataDecide configuration share three things. Those are the data recipe, the
      model size, and the hyperparameters. They differ in the seed, which sets initialisation and data order,
742
      and at every size below 1B the two nondefault seeds are trained by the suite to exactly a quarter of the
743
      1B compute budget. Their schedules are deliberately cut short. Within a configuration, the nonshared
744   environment is therefore the seed taken jointly with that budget gap, where a difference between
745   replicate runs stands in for that combination, and common-step scoring holds the evaluation point
746   fixed, while nothing holds the training schedule fixed, so the shortened schedule enters our estimate
747   as part of the nonshared environment, with no separate attribution to initialisation.
748
      Noise. Our estimand is the variance the seed leaves in a score. Our estimand is not the variance
749
      a benchmark has across models. The distinction matters because the second is dominated by real
750   differences in capability, which is exactly the signal an evaluation wants to keep, while the first is the
751   part that would move again under an exact repetition of the same training recipe.
752
753
      Across. Our estimate is a property of a whole battery instead of one
                                                                         √ benchmark in isolation. The
754   average of K benchmarks shrinks independent noise by exactly K. The       poff-diagonal of ΣE
755   says how much of that reduction a practitioner gets. It works through Λ = 1 + (K − 1)r̄E and
      Keff = K/Λ2 . The square of Λ is the design-effect analogue of survey sampling and Keff the


                                                         14
      Under review as a conference paper at ICLR 2027




756
      matching effective sample size (Kish, 1965), where a benchmark plays the role of a cluster member
757   and r̄E the role of the homogeneity rate inside a cluster, though the analogy is not an identity, because
758   cluster homogeneity in survey work is positive in nearly every application. It therefore holds Keff at
759   or below K. Our r̄E is estimated without bias near zero and can come out negative. So Keff runs
760   strictly above K wherever Λ̂ falls below 1. A single benchmark has no Λ, since the definition needs
761   something to average across.
762
      Phenotypes. The same items are scored on two scales. Those are the per-byte log-likelihood margin
763
      and the binary correctness it thresholds. We report both because they answer to different readers.
764
      Accuracy is the number a practitioner averages. The margin is the continuous quantity underneath
765   it that carries more information per item. We borrow the word from quantitative genetics, where a
766   phenotype is any measured trait of a unit and the decomposition into A, C, and E runs separately per
767   phenotype, and Section 2 imports that decomposition.
768
      Read together, SNAP names the cross-half estimator of how far a battery’s seed noise departs from
769
      the independent-benchmark idealisation, separately on each of the two scales. The null value is

1085   B.7   A DJACENT LITERATURE
1086
1087   Reimers & Gurevych (2017) report score distributions over seeds, dataset by dataset. Madaan et al.
1088   (2024) reports seed variance per benchmark and never across them. Hagmann et al. (2023) treat
1089   nondeterminism as measurement noise. The decomposition they give runs through linear mixed
1090   effects models, whose variance components are only scalars for the noise factors and for their
1091
       interaction with data properties. No cross-benchmark covariance term enters. Jordan (2024) attributes
       most test-set variance in standard trainings to per-example noise over seeds rather than to run-to-run
1092
       differences in distribution-level error, and his Appendix C does correlate run accuracies across five
1093
       ImageNet evaluation sets, at most R2 = 0.14, pairing shifted versions of one benchmark rather
1094   than distinct ones, while Bui et al. (2025) vary the fine-tuning seed of a fixed pretrained model,
1095   and Hochlehnert et al. (2025) the decoding seed. Both report instability benchmark by benchmark.
1096   Neither variance component is this paper’s E. Dodge et al. (2020) do look further on the fine-tuning
1097   seed, since their Section 4.3 asks whether a seed is good across datasets and reports that some weight
1098   initialisations do well on several GLUE tasks at once, though they read that pattern off four per-task
1099   rankings and estimate no correlation and no covariance. Across-dataset structure is a question they
1100   raise and leave open. Zhou et al. (2020) go further still on this point. Their Figure 3 prints a 15
1101   by 15 Spearman matrix over datasets, where the standard block sits at 0.95 and above while the
1102
       standard-to-analysis entries scatter from 0.97 for STR-L down to 0.30 for STR-O, and each entry is a
       Spearman correlation over one run’s checkpoints averaged across seeds, so competence differences
1103
       between checkpoints alone drive those entries. That makes their matrix an RP and not the RE we
1104
       estimate. Dufour et al. (2026) retrain several hundred image generators and split FID variance into
1105   training and sampling seeds. One metric admits no covariance. Miller (2024) gives item-level error
1106   bars for one model without reaching the training seed, and Zhao et al. (2026) compute a three-seed
1107   SD of a twelve-task average, and their Appendix E.4 does combine twelve per-task Welch tests,
1108   by treating each task as independent evidence rather than by estimating any relation between them.
1109   Haupt et al. (2026) come closest, since they rank benchmark items by welfare weight, improvability,
1110   and variance, and the variance term is drawn from the PolyPythias 410M panel we also use, and the
1111   within-checkpoint across-seed variance is pooled by their per-item noise estimate into one scalar
1112   for each item, while their own limitations state that the item-level tests carry an assumption of
1113
       approximate independence across items. Their panel is ours and their estimand is not, because a
       diagonal of per-item variances carries no off-diagonal correlation structure. They never estimate the
1114
       covariance matrix their own theory section defines.
1115
1116   A separate literature already counts the effective number of independent tests inside a correlated
1117   family. That count is the closest formal relative of our Keff . Cheverud (2001) derives an effective
1118   number of independent comparisons from the variance of the eigenvalues of an observed marker
1119
       correlation matrix, while Nyholt (2004) builds the same correction from a spectral decomposition of
       pairwise linkage disequilibrium. Li & Ji (2005) report a systematic overestimation in the first of the
1120
       two counts and give a more accurate one. All three take the correlation matrix as observed. All three
1121
       spend the count exclusively on a significance threshold rather than on an aggregate’s standard error.
1122   Our difficulty is the one they do not have, since no prior estimate of the seed-noise matrix exists in
1123   this literature to calibrate against. No release reports one, so the estimator has to come first. The
1124   count is what we read off it afterwards. Searle et al. (1992) give the standard account of the negative
1125   variance estimates a moment decomposition returns when a component sits near zero, which is what
1126   gate G3 reports on one margin trait and on two accuracy traits.
1127
       Metric choice carries a literature of its own, and Schaeffer et al. (2023) show that a metric with
1128   a discontinuity can manufacture an apparent jump where a continuous metric moves smoothly.
1129   Accuracy is our margin thresholded at zero. Our two phenotypes differ by exactly that transformation.
1130   We report both rather than keeping whichever one flatters the estimate. We do not read the accuracy
1131   null as a thresholding artefact, because Section 7 traces it instead to seed variance at or below item
1132   noise on seven of the ten traits. Dehghani et al. (2021) show that benchmark choice alone can reorder
1133   methods. That is a claim about the correlations a ranking inherits. Neither paper estimates a seed
       covariance.

       Other authors take up the reporting and comparison of results across several datasets at once, instead
1135   of the measurement of the noise inside one of them. Demšar (2006) and Dror et al. (2017) give the
1136   tests for a claim that spans multiple datasets. Card et al. (2020) shows how little power a typical
1137   comparison carries. Gorman & Bedrick (2019) measures how much a single standard split decides,
1138   and Burnell et al. (2023) argue for reporting per-instance results beside one aggregate, which is the
1139   same reporting question Henderson et al. (2018) and Agarwal et al. (2021) press in reinforcement
1140   learning, where the first shows how far a reported result moves with the random seed alone. The
1141   second proposes a stratified bootstrap that resamples runs independently inside a fixed task set, which
1142   widens an interval only for run noise and leaves cross-task dependence outside it. Their Appendix A.5
1143
       does add a variant that resamples tasks too, for the one-run-per-task regime, and tasks drawn as
       exchangeable still carry no covariance between them. None of them supplies the cross-benchmark
1144
       seed covariance. Each one either carries an assumption of independence across datasets or works
1145
       around the dependence without any measurement of it.

1147   Hardy et al. (2026) decompose benchmark variance across 4,000 released models with generalizability
1148   theory. The replication there runs over models instead of seeds. The same effective-count algebra
1149
       reaches raters in Kohli (2026), where nine frontier judges carry roughly two independent votes

1150
       benchmarks of one run. On the same side of the pipeline, Messing (2026) finds naive standard errors
1151
       40 to 60 percent too small once judge and prompt choice enter the variance.
1152
1153   Four older results in psychometrics supply the machinery we reuse. We name them because a reader
1154   from that field will recognise the estimator before they recognise the application. Spearman (1904)
1155
       corrects a correlation for attenuation by pairing two error-independent measurements of each thing.
       Our cross-half identity (2) is that correction carried to a covariance matrix, where half A and half B
1156
       of one item set play the two measurements. The correction lands on the diagonal as well as off it.
1157
       Cronbach (1951) reads a split-half coefficient as an estimate over random halves of an item universe,
1158   which is the reliability we tabulate per trait in Table 3, and which null N3 re-randomises 50 times.
1159   The split itself moves ΛM by only 0.002. Brown (1910) and Spearman (1910) give the prophecy
1160   formula for the reliability of a test lengthened k times, and its denominator 1 + (k − 1)ρ carries
1161   exactly the algebraic form of Λ2 , with items in the role our benchmarks take. The two are not one
1162   quantity. That ρ is a correlation between parallel items, while ours is a correlation between the seed
1163   effects on two benchmarks, so substitution of the battery means of Table 3 into that denominator
1164   at k = 10 returns 6.87 and 3.91, well above our own Λ2 . Campbell & Fiske (1959) arrange traits
1165   against methods in one matrix and read convergent and discriminant validity off its blocks, and our
1166   within-format against cross-format separation of RˆE in Section 5.2 is the same reading, where scoring
1167   format takes the method role and the benchmark the trait role. Only one of their two legs is open
1168
       to us here. No benchmark appears in two formats. We can therefore read method variance off the
       heterotrait blocks. We cannot read the monotrait convergent diagonal a fully crossed design would
1169
       supply. Brennan (2001) gives the variance-component framework that Hardy et al. (2026) apply
1170
       over released models, where our facet of generalisation is the training seed, not the rater and not
1171   the occasion. Item response theory offers the other modern route into item-level measurement, and
1172   Hofmann et al. (2025) fit an ability model per benchmark from existing evaluation results, on a panel
1173   overlapping ours and with seven authors in common with Heineman et al. (2025). A latent ability per
1174   benchmark carries no off-diagonal noise term, so that route reaches the same wall from the other side.
1175   Kipnis et al. (2024) do fit a common factor across six benchmarks, correlating at 0.94 with total score,
1176   and that is signal-side structure rather than the seed-noise covariance we need. Outside machine
1177   learning, Hedges et al. (2010) handle dependent effect sizes inside a cluster with a standard error that
1178   needs no knowledge of their covariance structure, where we estimate that correlation instead and
1179
       spend it on Keff , a point estimate a practitioner can act on rather than a robust band.

       D.3   P RACTICAL CONSIDERATIONS
1775          • Tc is computed on deviations, never on raw scores. With R = 3 a single high-competence
1776            seed would otherwise dominate every cross-half product. The estimator would return
1777            competence in place of its correlation. Within-configuration centring therefore carries the
1778            whole of the identification argument here (Remark 1).
1779          • The permutation null permutes independently per trait. A joint permutation of run
1780            labels across traits is not a null. It preserves every cross-trait pairing. Nor is a per-trait
1781            permutation clean, because one that happens to be the identity on every trait reproduces the
                observed value exactly. With R = 3 that identity has probability 6−K per configuration.

              • Gzip members are not seekable. A tarball member must be streamed from its start, so
1783            every run is read exactly once, and every quantity that might be needed is extracted on that
1784            pass, since a forgotten field costs a full re-read of the tarball.
1785
              • Token-budget batching. The step truncation at 530M and 750M is aligned with the default-

1842   Every Arm-2 number reported in this paper was produced on one card type. So a reader who
1843   attempts its reproduction elsewhere meets a floating-point question the design does not otherwise

1845   run leaves most items bit-identical, and the rescoring moves the mean absolute margin by 1.0 ×
1846   10−3 against a margin standard deviation of 0.391, a ratio of 2.6 × 10−3 , while the largest single
1847

1848

1857
1858   Remark 1. Tc must be formed on deviations, never on raw scores, since the cross-half product of
1859   two runs’ raw battery means is dominated by µ2c . A one-symbol slip there produces an inflation of
1860   Λ̂ by roughly three orders of magnitude. The failure mode is made structurally impossible by our
1861
       released code. The statistic is a projection onto an orthonormal basis of the mean-zero contrast space,
       and since every row of that basis annihilates the all-ones vector, the projection of the raw scores is
1862
       already the projection of the deviations. The property that buys the immunity admits verification. The
1863
       code asserts it unconditionally   at run time. That assertion requires every basis row to sum exactly to
1864                        P
       zero, and it requires c Tc to be unchanged when a per-configuration constant is added to every run
1865   of both halves.
