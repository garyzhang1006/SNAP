# Independent technical audit

Audit basis is the complete extracted text of the supplied 35-page PDF, including appendices. Locations below are printed PDF page and margin line numbers. No analysis code, original data, preregistration, or supplementary outputs were supplied to this audit, so empirical numbers remain reported values, not independently reproduced results. Mathematical counterexamples and arithmetic below were checked directly in Python.

## Priority corrections supported by the manuscript itself

1. **The claimed exact null expectation for Lambda is false.** Page 17, lines 873–879, derives E[T_perm] = U and then asserts E[Lambda_perm] = 1. The denominator is fixed by these permutations, so the derivation gives E[Lambda_perm squared] = 1. Concavity of the square root gives E[Lambda_perm] <= 1 when all squared ratios are nonnegative, with strict inequality for a nondegenerate distribution. For squared ratios 0.5 and 1.5 with equal probability, the mean squared ratio is 1 but the mean Lambda is 0.965926. This also invalidates “null value is exactly 1” as a finite-sample expectation on page 15, lines 768–770, and “rbar is estimated without bias” on page 15, line 759. The covariance estimator can be unbiased under its assumptions; a ratio and its square root generally aren't. Keep 1 as the population independence reference and call simulated centering an empirical calibration result. The full ratio, with random denominator, has another source of finite-sample bias.

2. **The reported rbar is not a normalized weighted mean correlation.** Page 16, lines 838–846, defines rbar = sum_(j!=k) r_jk sigma_j sigma_k / [(K-1) sum_j sigma_j squared]. This identity is useful, but the correlation weights generally don't sum to 1. For K=2, sigma=(1,2), and r_12=1, it returns 0.8 even though every off-diagonal correlation equals 1. Call this an effective covariance coefficient, and reserve “weighted mean correlation” for normalization by sum_(j!=k) sigma_j sigma_k. If both appear, define r_eff = a_sigma r_weighted, where a_sigma = sum_(j!=k) sigma_j sigma_k / [(K-1) sum_j sigma_j squared]. The headline bound 0.038 is therefore a bound on r_eff under the stated model and interval procedure, not on the average pairwise correlation. This matters because BoolQ supplies 84% of the accuracy trace.

3. **The jackknife intervals don't agree with the stated SEs at 95%.** Page 29, lines 1564–1565, gives SEs 0.049 and 0.039, then intervals 1.160–1.328 and 1.012–1.145. These use multipliers near 1.71, consistent with a two-sided 90% t interval for 24 degrees of freedom. Using the stated rounded SEs and t_24,0.975 approximately 2.0639 gives 1.143–1.345 and 0.998–1.159. Thus the accuracy jackknife check doesn't demonstrably exclude 1 at 95%, and the claim on page 30, lines 1566–1567, that both are inside the primary intervals isn't supported by this arithmetic. Recompute from unrounded estimates or remove these comparisons.

4. **The claimed lower bound from observed pair gaps is false without assumptions about true gaps.** Page 28, lines 1485–1491, calls 30.3% a lower bound on the fraction of configuration pairs with sufficiently small true gaps, because observed gaps contain seed noise. Noise can move a large true gap into the threshold as well as move a small gap out. For example, if every true gap exceeds the threshold, some noisy observed gaps can still fall below it, while the true fraction is zero. Report 455/1,500 as a descriptive count of estimated or observed gaps under a common noise model, not a lower bound. The formula for reversal probability on page 3, lines 142–143, also requires independent, approximately Gaussian run errors and equal variances; with unequal variances its denominator is sqrt(v_1+v_2), and with paired seeds covariance must enter. State these conditions before presenting the quarter-of-comparisons claim.

5. **The information-ratio derivation omits its distribution and local-shift assumptions.** Page 3, lines 149–156, says a shift delta changes accuracy by f(0) delta, which is a first-order approximation as delta approaches zero, not an exact finite-shift equality. Page 17, lines 891–895, sets f(0)=phi(z_0)/s_m, which assumes Gaussian item margins, or an explicitly stipulated Gaussian approximation. It does not follow from the mean and variance of arbitrary margins. Describe equation 5 as a Gaussian local-shift calculation. Its reported 1.61 is a plug-in model quantity computed from accuracy rates, not measured relative statistical information across the actual margin distributions. Preserve the admission that its lower gate is automatic under this formula.

6. **Disjoint halves alone don't establish the estimator identity.** Pages 3 and 15–16, lines 120–124 and 806–836, need zero conditional mean item errors, zero cross-half error covariance across all relevant run/trait terms, and exchangeable independent runs within a configuration. A fixed finite item bank split without replacement does not, by itself, supply those properties. Define the item-superpopulation target and the conditional independence assumption explicitly, distinguish fixed-item from item-general seed variability, and remove “holds however the measurement came out” and “needs no measurement model” on page 9, lines 449–452. A stochastic error model is already being assumed. The title's “free of item-sampling error” overstates what the admitted passage leakage permits.

7. **The full-set target mixes seed and training-schedule variation.** Page 14, lines 742–747, admits that scoring at a common step doesn't fix the training schedule. This conflicts with descriptions of the result as exact-repeat seed noise on lines 748–751. Common step removes a checkpoint-budget mismatch, but two schedules can give different learning rates and histories at that same step. The auxiliary-pair contrast is the cleaner seed comparison under shared auxiliary schedules, although source provenance still requires verification. Call the full-set result within-configuration run covariance, explicitly including documented schedule variation. Don't label the contrast “batch-free” as proof that all implementation differences vanished.

8. **The 6–61% gain range is not an identified bound.** Pages 8–9, lines 381–389 and 460–469; page 19, lines 1008–1014; and Table 13 show outcomes from two selected simulators at two gain settings. These are scenario sensitivities, not lower and upper bounds on the causal sharpness contribution in real training runs. Table 13 itself gives a 190.5% share at sigma_g=0.10, outside the advertised bound. No assumption supplied makes 0.042–0.05 an exhaustive plausible range, and no interval is provided for sigma_g. A multiplicative transformation of margins preserves accuracy by construction, so its accuracy result cannot validate a model of actual sharpness. Report simulated inflation under those specifications, not a bounded decomposition of the observed excess.

9. **A null not rejected does not establish that independence is adequate.** Pages 1, 5, 6, and 9 recommend averaging accuracy as independent, while the manuscript admits uncorrected leakage precisely in the trait dominating the denominator, strong composition sensitivity, and different results in the other population. The upper endpoint 1.157 gives a conditional sensitivity estimate, not an equivalence guarantee and not an unconditional upper bound if leakage biases Lambda toward 1. Replace the prescription with a narrow statement that this battery's raw-accuracy point estimate is near the independence reference, report the estimated seed SD and uncertainty, and recommend preserving covariance when replicate runs permit it. Raw accuracy uses equal benchmark weights; “equal weighting” for inverse-seed-SD standardization on pages 26–27 is potentially misleading and should be named standardized-trait weighting.

10. **The transport failure is scored on the wrong scale relative to the stated target.** Page 30, lines 1592–1596, acknowledges that the plan named sigma_agg, but the failure is called from the excess Lambda-1 instead. The reported ratio 1.40574/1.24395 is about 1.130, within 20%, whereas their excess ratio is 1.663. A deliberately unfavorable post hoc definition remains post hoc and doesn't make the inference valid. Report the two scales as exploratory sensitivities, and only score a sigma_agg transport criterion from predicted and measured sigma_agg on a matched target. Both primary text and Table 6 should avoid calling the originally stated prediction falsified from this surrogate scale.

## Further technical and acceptance risks

- **Preregistration status contradicts the paper's confirmatory vocabulary.** Page 10, lines 523–528, says the plan is absent, never hashed, and its asserted date uncorroborated. Page 5, lines 216–224, says the intended holdout was never drawn and all decisions used the full population. Page 4, line 170, nevertheless calls Prediction 1 confirmatory. Use exploratory throughout and call the appendix an author-reported analysis plan. Enumeration of subsets bounds the fixed statistic under subsets; it cannot undo decisions made with the full data visible, nor prove what the screening-driven workflow would have selected.
- **The asymptotic floor needs convergence conditions.** Page 16, lines 844–846, claims bounded variances suffice for the limit. They don't imply that either average variance or the effective covariance coefficient converges as K grows. State a conditional limit when both converge, or retain only the exact finite-K identity. No empirical claim about arbitrarily large benchmark batteries follows from ten traits.
- **Gain residualization and competence residualization aren't identified mechanisms here.** Page 17, lines 903–917, admits the competence proxy is dominated by a trait with a floored negative variance, and that the gain null doesn't reproduce the realized covariate's cross-half noise. Keep residualization descriptive and remove any suggestion it rules out competence or sharpness. A new proxy requires rerunning the analysis, not rewriting the interpretation alone.
- **The permutation check's exchangeability and clustering claims conflict.** Page 23, lines 1240–1241, says per-trait within-configuration reassignment carries none of the recipe clustering, while page 29, lines 1543–1546, says it holds recipe clustering at observed values. It preserves configuration-specific marginal score magnitudes and cluster membership, but independently relabeling each cell can destroy dependence of seed effects across cells within a recipe. Specify the exact conditional null and what dependence remains. A finite null coverage experiment is not validation at the observed non-null parameter.
- **Identity permutations belong in the permutation sample space.** Page 33, lines 1779–1781, calls a per-trait permutation unclean because the identity can reproduce the observation. That is false as a general objection. Uniform permutation tests ordinarily include the identity. The concern about one joint permutation preserving cross-trait pairing is correct, but the two claims must be separated. No code was inspected to establish whether the implementation excludes any transformations.
- **Correlation-matrix estimates can fall outside the parameter space, but spectral claims then need restraint.** Page 23, lines 1218–1226, reports accuracy correlations above 1 and a negative eigenvalue. The unprojected covariance moment estimator can have this sampling behavior, but its normalized correlations are not themselves unbiased merely because covariance moments are. Spectral participation ratios on an indefinite matrix aren't counts of independent noise directions. Keep this diagnostic separate from population rank claims.
- **A shared clipping convention can affect model rankings.** Page 31, lines 1629–1633, says clipping negative held-out variances affects every model “rather than ranking them.” The same variance vector interacts differently with different correlation matrices, so shared clipping does not establish ranking invariance. Also same-data target variance and supplied held-out diagonal variances are dependent. Describe the experiment as conditional prediction with estimated held-out scales and report no inferential winner without paired uncertainty.
- **Neither the jackknife composition intervals nor quadrature additions are calibrated inference.** Page 27, lines 1419–1437, treats a trait jackknife and sampling SE as additive in quadrature, then calls the result conservative. Nonrandom benchmark choice, varying K, shared data, and unknown covariance prevent that claim without a model. Keep leave-one-trait-out ranges as sensitivity summaries and delete formal composition intervals or label them illustrative arithmetic only.
- **Point comparisons aren't uncertainty comparisons.** Page 8, lines 395–407, uses “earns nothing” and “indistinguishable” with no interval. Write that one model has a smaller reported mean error and that uncertainty in the difference wasn't estimated. Likewise a finite point estimate below a proposed threshold doesn't establish the population threshold is false.
- **Citation coverage needs its own primary-source audit.** The references include numerous 2026 works and strong claims that nobody has estimated the target. Their existence, metadata, and relationship to the claimed novelty weren't independently checked in this subtask. Absence of a comparison in a supplied release is weaker than absence throughout the literature.
- **Reproducibility assertions need real artifacts.** Page 10 and Appendix D claim a complete supplied supplement, manually re-derived equations, full citation audit, and successful checks. Those are author attestations and must not be carried into a revised final paper as facts unless the corresponding assets and checks are verified. The AI statement's denial of AI-generated synthetic datasets entering any reported number also needs clarification against AI-designed/implemented simulation nulls that do enter tables, without inventing an account of how they were made.

## What appears sound within explicit assumptions

The cross-half covariance estimator with within-configuration centering and division by R-1 is a valid method-of-moments identity under the stated zero-error-covariance and i.i.d. run conditions. Negative estimated diagonal moments alone are not proof the method is invalid. Pooling numerator and denominator before division is preferable to averaging unstable cell ratios, although it doesn't make the pooled ratio unbiased. The finite-K variance decomposition, when rbar is correctly named and defined, is algebraically sound. Using the reported upper accuracy endpoint 1.157 gives r_eff approximately 0.037628 and K_eff approximately 7.470, so those numerical conversions are correct conditional on that endpoint. The main scientific opportunity is a clear, exploratory measurement study of within-configuration covariance and sensitivity to metric scale and benchmark composition, with claims limited by identified assumptions and reproduced evidence.
