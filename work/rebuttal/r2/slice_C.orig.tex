\section{Results}
\subsection{Full-battery estimates and composition}
\label{sec:composition}
Full-battery margins exclude independence, while accuracy gives only a bound. Inflation reaches 1.244 on margins, where the raw interval excludes one, and 1.078 on accuracy, where it includes one (Table~\ref{tab:primary}). At the accuracy upper limit of 1.157 this fixed battery has $\bar r_E$ of 0.038 and $K_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of 7.47 under the stated measurement model.

Accuracy carries little seed signal at the benchmark level, where reliability averages 0.324 against 0.652 on margins, and Table~\ref{tab:original3} and Appendix~\ref{app:supplementary} give the per-benchmark diagnostics, none of which enter the headline ratio because it uses covariance products directly.

The factor belongs to this battery, since margin correlations track scoring format, averaging 0.624 within formats and 0.007 across them (Figure~\ref{fig:covariance}), and BoolQ, the only benchmark in its format, carries 68\% of the margin covariance trace and 84\% of the accuracy trace. Removing it raises margin inflation to 1.786 with interval \ci{1.689}{1.877}, and accuracy inflation to 1.558 with interval \ci{1.412}{1.687}, while removing CommonsenseQA instead gives inflation estimates of 1.096 and 1.014. The removal estimates imply off-diagonal terms involving BoolQ summing to about $-0.15$ of the margin trace and $-0.06$ of the accuracy trace. Its removal lowers the denominator of Equation~\ref{eq:lambda} more than the numerator, which raises the factor while the margin aggregate standard deviation falls from 0.00572 to 0.00516, because the factor compares each battery with independence at its own marginal variances. Lowering BoolQ's weight from a tenth to 0.0867 already stops the accuracy interval including one (Appendix~\ref{app:supplementary}).

Shared passages don't account for BoolQ's trace share, although that check can't reach other shared item effects. We recovered the 2,938 passages behind BoolQ's 3,270 questions from the release's request files, and assigning each passage whole to one half changes the two estimates by $-0.0002$ and $-0.0001$ on average across fifty re-splits. The BoolQ diagonal moves by 0.03\% and 0.15\% (Appendix~\ref{app:derivations}). A permutation that reshuffles each run's item deviations inside every group leaves all trait scores fixed and breaks the tie between particular items and runs. Across 2,000 such permutations 0.087 reach or exceed the observed margin estimate and 0.352 the observed accuracy estimate. Item-level sharing accounts for about 0.001 of the margin estimate and 0.002 of the accuracy estimate, but an effect carried by every item of a benchmark survives this permutation untouched.

Benchmark removal moves our estimates more than recipe or size removal. Recipe removal moves margin inflation between 1.207 and 1.256, and size removal between 1.178 and 1.289, where the deletion of 530M gives \ci{0.993}{1.338} and stops excluding one. The corresponding accuracy ranges run from 1.065 to 1.099 and from 1.047 to 1.115. Among equal-weight subsets of the same size, those containing BoolQ average 0.115 lower margin inflation at two benchmarks and 0.596 lower at nine than those without it. A sweep of 2,000 weightings from a flat Dirichlet leaves 0.817 of margin intervals excluding one. Figure~\ref{fig:composition} shows every single-benchmark removal with its interval on both scales, and Appendix~\ref{app:composition} separates benchmark count, identity, and weights. Our design treats this battery as fixed, and that spread isn't a distribution over benchmark collections.

\subsection{A registered test on unseen tasks}
\label{sec:heldout}
We find that the registered rule fails on held-out tasks. Held-out margin inflation on the 225 runs at 530M, 750M, and 1B is 1.217 with interval \ci{0.872}{1.498}, while the original ten on the same runs give 1.240 with interval \ci{1.054}{1.397} and pass it, a comparison we fixed before any held-out score existed. However, the two point estimates differ by 0.024, the held-out interval is 0.626 wide against 0.343 for the original, and our rule reads the lower limit. Interval width rather than the point gap decides the registered verdict.

Because the ten benchmarks above shaped our estimator, we registered this test on tasks outside them before any of their production scores existed. The protocol named SciQ, MedMCQA, and two AGIEval reasoning tasks, with a rule replacing any task whose 1B pilot accuracy fell below chance plus 0.02, a bar both AGIEval tasks missed by about a thousandth, inside the pilot's sampling error of about 0.010. The battery became SciQ, MedMCQA, and multiple-choice versions of DROP and CoQA, with 1,000 items in each of the four tasks and items sharing a passage or story kept in the same half. Our GPU budget couldn't cover all 375 released checkpoints, and our protocol's scope rule limited the test to those runs at the steps of the primary analysis. The test passes only if the lower 95\% limit exceeds one, and Table~\ref{tab:heldout} reports the test at its registered scope. Every partial scope we read before the full one also failed the rule, with held-out margin estimates between 1.152 and 1.203, and Appendix~\ref{app:heldout} lists all five with the scoring hardware and a power simulation that can't rescue the test.

As an exploratory result, the held-out accuracy inflation interval excludes one. Inflation is 1.220 with interval \ci{1.066}{1.359}, above the 1.097 that the original ten give at the same scope. Removing CoQA lowers held-out margin inflation to 1.090, while removing any other task leaves it between 1.120 and 1.213, so the held-out estimate depends on battery composition as the original does (Appendix~\ref{app:heldout}).
