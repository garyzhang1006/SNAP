## Questions reviewers would ask

1. Which variance does a practitioner need: run variance on the fixed released items, or the item-general run covariance SNAP estimates? What is the fixed-bank inflation on the same 375 runs? (R1, R2)
2. Why estimate cross-half covariance at all when the recommended practice is to compute the SD of the battery average across replicates directly? What does SNAP add in that setting? (R1, R3)
3. Given a wrong-call change of at most 0.009 and independence winning out of sample, in what concrete situation does ignoring covariance lead a researcher to a wrong conclusion? (AC, R3)
4. Is the 1.244 driven by seed noise or by schedule differences between default and auxiliary runs, given the 750M truncation and the 33-configuration estimate of 1.082 with an interval from 0.121? (R2, R4)
5. How large a shared item component is plausible, and why did you not score DataDecide on bank two, since that family carries the headline? (R2, R4)
6. Why keep a PolyPythias rule with power 0.356 as confirmatory, and what interval width do you expect? (R2)
7. Could bank two's train split carry pretraining contamination for Pythia that changes run covariance on that half only? (R4)
8. Does the format structure (0.624 within, 0.007 across) hold for generative or exact-match tasks, where there is no answer-likelihood margin? (R3)
9. What changed between the registered protocol and the executed test, with dates, and how many scopes were run before the final one? (R4)
10. How does this relate to Miller (2024) and to the signal-to-noise analysis of Heineman et al. (2025) on the same checkpoints? (R1)

## Three text-only changes that would most raise the average

1. Resolve the practical-significance contradiction (AR1, AR14, AR20, AR21). State in the introduction and discussion that shared seed labels make paired recipe comparisons cancel most run covariance, so covariance matters for unpaired comparisons and single-model error bars, and give the one-sentence rule for each case. This converts the paper's most damaging number (0.009) into a usable finding and is the change most likely to move R1, R3, and the AC by one step.
2. Rewrite the abstract and first page around a stated thesis and cut to nine pages (AR8, AR9, AR19, AR17). About 200 words, no process disclaimers, genetics vocabulary moved to the appendix, and the proxy, planning-threshold, and Heineman paragraphs moved out of the main text. This removes a desk-reject risk and raises presentation scores across every seat.
3. Make the estimand and identification explicit in Section 2 (AR2, AR3, AR4). Name item-general versus fixed-bank run covariance, say which question each answers, present the 0.124 share as a sensitivity parameter next to the cross-format constraint, and frame 1.244 as this battery's value of a format-structured covariance. This answers the question R2 and R4 will open with.

## Experiments reviewers would demand

1. Score the 375 DataDecide runs on the disjoint second bank, so the shared-item check runs in the family that supplies the headline (acknowledged in limitations as unrun for lack of GPU time).
2. Report fixed-bank run inflation (SD of the battery average across replicates, no split) on the same runs beside the cross-half estimate. This needs only saved item scores, so it may be CPU-only.
3. An unpaired decision analysis: repeat the wrong-call experiment with seed-mismatched pairs (for example recipe A seed 2 against recipe B seed 14) to show where the covariance changes calls. Also CPU-only on saved scores.
4. The held-out test at all five sizes and all 375 runs, since the registered scope was set by GPU budget and failed on interval width.
5. A generative or exact-match task set (GSM8K, TriviaQA-style) on the same checkpoints, to test whether the format structure extends beyond answer-likelihood margins.
6. Any multi-seed population above 1B, or an explicit statement that none is public (appendix B.6 already argues this for Pythia, OLMo-2, and MultiBERTs).
