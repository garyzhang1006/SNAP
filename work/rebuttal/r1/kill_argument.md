## Attack memo (Codex thread 1, verbatim)

> Reject. Paper's headline result is not identified as run-to-run covariance. Title promises "Estimating Run-to-Run Covariance Across Language Model Benchmarks" (`main.tex:14`), and abstract says item-sampling noise "drops out" (`main.tex:29`), but estimator itself requires cross-half item errors to have zero covariance (`main.tex:70-75`) and paper concedes item noise shared across benchmarks "would also enter the numerator" (`main.tex:75`). This is fatal because same section says estimate is "identified only up to that component" (`main.tex:75`), while Discussion admits scalar shared item effects can reproduce both headline values, including 1.244 (`main.tex:271`). Thus central number is compatible with non-run covariance.
>
> Empirical design does not rescue identification. Primary DataDecide runs mix seed with checkpoint and schedule variation: training schedules differ (`main.tex:67`), truncation concentrates in one band (`main.tex:131`), and authors read accuracy only "conditional on the checkpoint we scored" (`main.tex:133`). Planned screening was never formed, all choices saw all 125 configurations, and no independent timestamp exists (`main.tex:135`). The only registered fresh-task test fails its rule (`main.tex:43`, `main.tex:183`). The paper then admits every analysis apart from that failed test is exploratory (`main.tex:49`) and that the shared-item check was not run in the family supplying the headline (`main.tex:271`).

My ruling on the net assessment is that the attack survives as a scope attack but falls short of a validity kill. Line 75 states the identification condition and line 271 quantifies the item share that would be needed, which puts the paper ahead of most empirical submissions. The exposure sits in the title and in the abstract's first two sentences, which a reviewer reads before line 75. The sec:family cross-bank rule is the paper's intended answer to P1, so its design (KA4, KA6, KA14) is where rebuttal effort pays most.

## Findings

| id | severity | likely to lower score | location (section, verbatim quote from main.tex) | reviewer's objection | suggested fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KA1 | critical | yes | Abstract, "so item-sampling noise drops out"; Sec 2.2, "we therefore read our estimate as identified only up to that component" | The abstract and title claim run covariance while Section 2.2 concedes the numerator also carries any item effect shared across benchmarks and repeated across halves, and 0.124 of item variance reproduces 1.244. | Rewrite the abstract's second sentence so it names the condition ("item noise not shared across halves drops out") and gives the 0.124 share, and add "item-general" before "run covariance" in the title or first contribution sentence. |
| KA2 | major | yes | Sec 3, "On the 33 configurations that share a final step, margin inflation is 1.082" | The only subset free of checkpoint truncation gives an interval that includes one, so the headline could be schedule variation rather than seed variation. | Say in the abstract that the estimand is covariance of released runs at the selected checkpoints, give the width or power of the 33-configuration cut so readers see it as uninformative, and point to PolyPythias (clean reruns at one step) as the schedule-free check. |
| KA3 | major | yes | Sec 1, "Every analysis apart from the registered test is exploratory." | With the screening split never drawn, no corroborated timestamps and the one registered test failed, no DataDecide number is confirmatory. | Lead the contributions with the estimator and its calibrated interval, and present 1.244 as one exploratory measurement, which the paper already does in places but not in the abstract's order. |
| KA4 | major | yes | Sec 4.3, "It supports the explanation when the difference is positive and the cross-bank interval covers one" | "Positive" is undefined (point estimate or interval), no operating characteristics are given for the second rule, and with bank two at a sixth of bank one a cross-bank interval covering one is likely from noise alone, so "supported" can fire without a shared-item component. | Define "positive" as the lower jackknife limit above zero if the committed rule says so, and report the simulated branch probabilities of rule two under no shared-item component and under a 0.124 share, as line 213 already does for rule one. |
| KA5 | major | yes | Sec 4.3, "which means the rule can confirm the effect in this family but a failure can't count against it" | A rule with 0.356 power whose failure cannot count against the effect gives the replication no way to hurt the paper, which reads as unfalsifiable. | State what result would count against transfer to this family (for example an upper jackknife limit below a named value), and keep the 0.356 figure next to it. |
| KA6 | major | yes | Sec 4.3 (R2 supported branch), "is the one we'd report for its run covariance" | The confound caveat (banks differ in split, item count and exemplar overlap) appears only in the not-supported branch, yet the supported branch is where the cross-bank value gets adopted as the run covariance. | Move the sentence "The two banks also differ in split, in item count, and in exemplar overlap..." from line 219 into the rule paragraph at line 213 so it covers every branch. |
| KA7 | major | yes | Sec 4.3 (R2 not-supported branch), "can't account for the inflation we measure on PolyPythias" | A within-minus-cross interval that includes zero at a sixth of the items is a failure to detect a difference, and the sentence reads it as exclusion. | Replace with "gives no evidence that a component the halves of one item set share accounts for the inflation", and give the difference interval's upper limit as the bound. |
| KA8 | critical | yes | End of main text, "\label{maintext:end}" | Roughly 1.1 pages over the 9-page limit with one outcome selected is a desk-reject risk, independent of content. | Cut the abstract from about 330 words (one variant selected) to about 200 by dropping the test-inversion endpoint, the 6.5-benchmark restatement and the BoolQ-removal number, and move the external-checks paragraph (Sec 4.4, second paragraph) and Table 4 to the appendix. |
| KA9 | major | yes | Sec 4.1, "A larger factor accompanies a less variable average." | Because removing BoolQ raises inflation from 1.244 to 1.786 while lowering the aggregate standard deviation, the headline ratio is not the quantity a practitioner cares about. | Report the aggregate standard deviation ratio against independence as the practitioner number in the abstract, and keep the ratio as the battery descriptor with this caveat beside it. |
| KA10 | major | yes | Sec 1, "declares a difference in 0.113 of replicates" | The motivating 0.113 false-positive rate is simulated, while on real recipe comparisons the correction changes wrong calls by at most 0.009 with every interval reaching zero. | Put the 0.009 result in the same intro paragraph as the 0.113 simulation so the motivation does not rest on the simulated number alone. |

The single most damaging argument is KA1 combined with KA2. The title and abstract promise run-to-run covariance, yet Section 2.2 concedes that a shared item effect of 0.124 of item variance reproduces the headline, and the one truncation-free subset gives an interval of 0.121 to 1.530. The only test of the item alternative (rule two) runs on a different family, with a bank a sixth the size and a "supported" branch whose threshold is undefined.
