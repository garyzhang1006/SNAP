## Attack memo (codex thread 1, verbatim)

> Reject: central claim not established. SNAP estimates run covariance only under zero cross-half item-error covariance; authors explicitly admit disjoint item identifiers do not establish that condition and shared passages or duplicate content can connect halves (deliverables/main.tex:66-71; deliverables/appendix_a.tex:41-56). Yet headline 1.244 is presented as item-general covariance that transfers beyond released items, while primary analysis used a fixed DataDecide bank, after every primary choice could see all 125 configurations and no holdout survived (deliverables/main.tex:123). Validation that could separate true run covariance from bank-specific structure is missing: authors did not score DataDecide on second bank, leaving 1.244 untested on fresh items and shared-item check unrun in family supplying headline (deliverables/main.tex:235). The one pre-specified held-out task test fails its own rule, 1.217 [0.872, 1.498] (deliverables/main.tex:171-187). The promised second-family evidence is still unresolved outcome machinery, with PolyPythias values left as pending variants (deliverables/main.tex:43, 205-213). Even operational payoff is weak: corrected vs independent rules change wrong-call rates by at most 0.009 and intervals reach zero (deliverables/main.tex:222). Thus the paper's title-level contribution reduces to an exploratory fixed-battery observation on one release, not a validated estimator or empirical result "across language model benchmarks."

Codex net assessment (verbatim, one sentence): "Best-case PolyPythias outcome could lift it to borderline or weak reject, around ICLR 4-5. Current pending state or any R1 fail / R2 supported outcome reads more like reject, around 3-4."

My net assessment: the attack lands because of how the paper frames itself, not because of a defect in the estimator. Section 2 sells an item-general, bank-transferable target that is never tested on fresh items in the headline family, while the paper's own recommendation (main.tex:239) is the fixed-bank replicate SD, which gives 1.237 and needs neither the split nor the independence assumption. If the contributions paragraph led with the fixed-battery result, stated the untested transfer as untested, and gave one concrete number for what the split adds (accuracy without BoolQ, where item noise is 0.578 of the diagonal and the split gives 1.558 against the fixed-bank 1.285, main.tex:63), then P1 to P3 lose most of their force at no compute cost. P4 and P5 can't be fixed with text. The most they allow is honest framing, and the held-out failure plus a likely R1 fail is what caps the score.
