## Field analysis

The paper sits in ML evaluation methodology, specifically the statistics of benchmark aggregation. Its method is a split-half moment estimator taken from psychometrics and quantitative genetics, applied to DataDecide's released item scores (375 runs, 10 benchmarks) and to PolyPythias (45 runs). The contribution is a measurement plus an estimator. At ICLR the paper goes to evaluation and datasets reviewers, and they will read it with the signal-and-noise work of Heineman et al. (2025) and Madaan et al. (2024) in mind. The paper is mature and very carefully audited, and it deliberately under-claims.

The R2 verdict matters less to these scores than R1 does, because not supported and undecided read the same to most reviewers.

**R1 (5/4).** The seat credits the estimator, the recipe-clustered inference, and the thorough deletion sweeps, and it finds the question of cross-benchmark run covariance well motivated. Its objections are that the number measured belongs to this one battery (BoolQ holds 68% of the trace, and single removals give 1.096 to 1.786), and that the pre-specified held-out test failed. After both, the seat is unsure what general claim is left.

**R2 (6/4).** This seat likes the coverage study (twelve populations, reruns at 10,000 replicates, the test-inversion and Beran checks), the honest treatment of pooled-ratio bias, and the permutation and band-sharing bounds. It marks the paper down for the checkpoint and schedule confound in DataDecide and because the identifying assumption (no item component shared across halves) is only tested in the second family, where that test has low power.

**R3 (3/3).** The seat's question is what a practitioner should do differently. The paper's own answer is to report the replicate SD of the battery average, which needs no SNAP split, and the correction changes at most 12 of 173 to 223 real recipe calls. The accuracy scale is the one practitioners report, and there the result is a bound at 0.135 power. The seat also finds the prose too dense with numbers to pull out a takeaway.

**R4 (3/4).** This seat notes that the pre-specified held-out test failed, that the planning predictions failed, and that the analysis plan's split was never drawn. The pre-registration also has no verifiable timestamp. The PolyPythias rules come with an exploratory reading that was added after the rules were fixed, and the power of rule 1 is 0.259. The seat counts all of this against the paper, even though the disclosure is exemplary.

## Top weaknesses (consensus)

1. The practical effect is small. Real recipe decisions barely change, and the headline size error (0.113) comes from simulation (AR1).
2. The fixed-bank replicate SD gives 1.237, against 1.244 from the split, so the titled estimator adds little to the recommendation the paper ends on (AR2).
3. The inflation factor belongs to the battery and the weighting. It depends heavily on BoolQ, and the paper tells readers to measure their own battery, which limits how far the number transfers (AR3).
4. The evidence meant to confirm the result is weak. The held-out test failed, the plan predictions failed, and the second-family rule has 0.259 power (AR4, AR5).
5. Identification is untested where the headline comes from. Neither a format-following item component in DataDecide nor the cross-bank check at a component size that could explain 1.244 has been examined (AR6).

## Questions reviewers will ask

1. Where the fixed-bank replicate SD and the split disagree, which one should a practitioner report, and why? Can you show a battery in which the choice changes a conclusion?
2. What is rule 1's power when item noise comes from the observed 14M to 410M PolyPythias reliabilities (the 4,755-item transfer run already exists) instead of DataDecide's?
3. How does rule 2 behave when a shared item component is large enough to produce 1.244? Also, what verdict should a reader draw from "not supported" given that the simulation never reaches that regime?
4. Is there a single practitioner-facing number, such as an SE multiplier or a run count, that holds across reasonable batteries? Or is the recommendation always to measure it yourself?
5. At 1B, where 24 configurations share a final step, what is the estimate, and does it answer the checkpoint confound?
6. Can the protocol and rule commits be tied to a public, third-party timestamp?

## Three text-only changes that would most raise the mean

1. **Rewrite the framing around the practitioner consequence, and cut the abstract's numbers.** The abstract now carries about 20 numbers and five intervals. Open instead with the two sentences a practitioner needs: independence understates the battery-average SE by about 24% on margins for this battery, and the fix is to report the replicate SD with a removal check. After that, say what the split adds. State in the intro that the decision-level effect on DataDecide is small and explain why (most recipe gaps are large), so reviewers do not find it on their own in Section 4.5. This targets R3 and R1 and is worth about +1 on R3.
2. **Motivate the split with the case where it diverges from the fixed-bank SD.** Accuracy without BoolQ gives 1.285 fixed-bank against 1.558 from the split, with item noise at 0.578 of the diagonal, and that belongs in the intro as the reason SNAP exists. Then list what only the split gives: per-benchmark run variances, the redrawn-bank target, and K_eff for run planning. This answers the question "why does the title name a method the recommendation does not need?" and should move R1 and R3.
3. **Put every deviation from the plan into one table, and anchor the pre-registration.** A single main-text or early-appendix table would list the plan item, the deviation, when it was decided (before or after seeing data), and its effect on the headline. It replaces the scattered post-hoc admissions, such as "We picked it after seeing the first four cells" at appendices_bcd.tex:253. Where they exist, cite the public commit SHAs and push dates for the protocol and the rule commits. The same disclosure then reads as rigor instead of repeated confession, which moves R4 and R2.

## Experiments reviewers would demand

1. Score the 375 DataDecide runs on bank two so the cross-bank identification check runs in the family that produces the headline. The paper names this gap itself at main.tex:235.
2. Measure a false-positive rate on real null comparisons: use the nine PolyPythias seeds of one configuration as null pairs, and compare the rejection rate of the independence SE with the corrected SE. This replaces the simulated 0.113 with an observed number.
3. Run a larger or more varied battery, for example 20 or more OLMES tasks, including one or two multi-format benchmarks, to show how the factor changes with composition and whether the two-correlation format fit predicts a new battery out of sample.
4. Estimate at matched final checkpoints: either 1B only, where 24 configurations share a final step, or rescore the 750M default runs at their released finals, to remove the schedule and truncation confound.
5. Take a competence measurement from outside the benchmarks (held-out loss at the scored checkpoints). The paper shows all 375 checkpoints can be reached, at appendices_bcd.tex:316.
6. Test beyond 1B or on generative scoring, even with one additional multi-seed population. Reviewers will ask for this, but the paper already explains why no such population exists.
