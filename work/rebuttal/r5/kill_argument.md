## Attack memo (verbatim, codex thread 1)

> Reject. The paper's headline says it estimates run-to-run covariance "Across Language Model Benchmarks" (main.tex:14), but its central DataDecide claim rests on an unidentified split-half estimand. The identity requires zero cross-half item-error covariance (appendix_a.tex:41), yet the appendix admits that item identifiers cannot verify this and that fixed-bank splitting needs an additional sampling model (appendix_a.tex:54). The problem is not cosmetic: outside BoolQ, repeated stems remain ungrouped (appendix_a.tex:56), and the authors did not run the disjoint-bank identification check on the DataDecide family that supplies the headline 1.244 result (main.tex:235). Meanwhile, the confirmatory held-out task test fails its own lower-limit rule (main.tex:171-175, main.tex:186), and the original analysis plan left no heldout split for primary choices (main.tex:123). The observed effect is also battery-specific: BoolQ carries most covariance trace and benchmark removal moves estimates from 1.096 to 1.786 (main.tex:157), while format and task content are explicitly inseparable (main.tex:215-216). This leaves a paper whose advertised covariance measurement depends on assumptions it cannot check, fails its main fresh-task validation, and then generalizes from one fixed, format-confounded battery to benchmark evaluation practice.

Net assessment. The paper survives the memo as it was written, because the body discloses nearly every point the memo raises. The memo still costs the paper because the abstract leads with the split-based 1.244 and a cross-format "exclusion" that is weaker than it reads, and because the memo missed the attack most likely to decide an ICLR score, which is significance: the recommended practice needs no SNAP, and the correction barely changes real decisions (KA3). The two internal problems I found (KA1, KA2) are fixable in text and should go first.
