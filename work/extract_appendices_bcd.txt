Variation across model sizes

The intervals don't support a ranking of model sizes. All five margin and four accuracy point estimates exceed one. Prediction 5 passes a point-estimate reading and fails the stricter interval reading of the original scorecard.

The cluster-robust mathematical-expression interval on the log of the squared ratio gives 0p811 to 1p628. The configuration percentile bootstrap gives 0p834 to 1p643. The wild bootstrap on the log of the squared ratio gives 0p491 to 1p656.

A separate run scored those constructions inside one size band at the three runs the release ships, and the transform doesn't repair that case. On an accuracy-like band the log interval misses in 0p151 of replicates against 0p017 for the plain one. It buys definedness on that band at a cost in coverage. The 1B log-scale endpoints should therefore be read as closer to a ninety percent statement than a ninety five percent one (seed 20260972, 4,000 replicates). Simulation says these per-size rows should be read as estimates with wide uncertainty, since they don't carry their nominal level as intervals. The accuracy column is the weaker of the two. One band at three runs covers 0p930 on a margin-like population and only 0p707 on an accuracy-like one, and ten runs lift the accuracy case to 0p947. Power inside a single band is worse again, since at a true margin ratio of 1p2446 a single band excludes one in 0p313 of replicates at three runs, 0p739 at six and 0p957 at ten. At a true accuracy ratio of 1p078 the exclusion rate reaches only 0p081 at ten runs and 0p317 at forty. No affordable run count settles the accuracy question inside one band, which is why the paper draws its conclusions from the pooled design (4,000 replicates, seeds 20260963 and 20260971).

Permutation comparison
N2 was proposed as an estimator-bias check and was never the primary inferential procedure. In the reported 500 permutations, none reaches the observed margin estimate, and 0p028 reach the accuracy estimate. The observed configurations are held fixed under this reassignment, while trait-specific run labels are permuted independently. The primary bootstrap instead targets variation across recipe clusters. The two procedures use different randomisation and sampling assumptions, which is why we report both accuracy results.

Associations with scoring format
 
The cross-format diagnostic retains the diagonal sum and only those off-diagonal terms whose traits have different formats. It has a null reference of one when those retained covariances vanish, but the masked sum needn't represent the variance of a realisable benchmark average. Because each benchmark has only one format, the comparison can't identify a causal effect of format.

The separate calculation reproduces the full estimate to four decimal places and then retains 54 of the 90 ordered off-diagonal pairs. It gives 0p921 with interval 0p801 to 1p035 for margins, and 0p965 with interval 0p848 to 1p063 for accuracy, both below the proposed 1p2 threshold. The margin result falls below the 1p153 expected from uniformly distributed covariance contributions under the comparison used here, and below the 1p113 that a uniform shared item component with the whole margin excess alone, with no seed covariance, would imply, a value the interval also excludes (Appendix 1 ).

All of BoolQ's off-diagonal pairs cross a format boundary, since it is the only yes/no benchmark. Its margin seed variance is mathematical-expression , compared with mathematical-expression for the next-largest trait. Without BoolQ, the cross-format margin diagnostic is 1p006 with interval 0p972 to 1p039, compared with an unrestricted nine-trait estimate of 1p786 with interval 1p689 to 1p877.

Among the nine traits with defined margin correlations, without WinoGrande, the mean correlation is 0p624 across sixteen within-format pairs and 0p007 across twenty cross-format pairs. The three cloze traits give margin inflation of 1p225 with interval 1p064 to 1p367. Without ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge in the multiple-choice block, inflation is 1p479 with interval 1p416 to 1p541. These associations show concentration within the larger format groups. Benchmark content and format, however, remain confounded in this battery. A rank-one model with trait-specific loadings can also produce uneven contributions across those groups.

Performance correlation as a noise proxy
The reported Frobenius distances between mathematical-expression and mathematical-expression are 5p59 for margins and 6p49 for accuracy. Negative estimated variances leave eighteen margin and 34 accuracy ordered off-diagonal correlations undefined. The implementation fills these entries with zero before it takes differences, and that includes undefined diagonal entries compared with unit diagonals in mathematical-expression . Those diagonal substitutions contribute 1p0 and 2p0 to the squared distances.

A repair of only those diagonals gives distances of 5p499 and 6p339. If we restrict the comparison to jointly defined entries, the distances are 4p374 and 4p593, still above the planned threshold of 0p5. The threshold alone discriminates little between ten-trait matrices. The entrywise comparisons provide a more useful description.

The off-diagonal association between the two matrices is 0p756 for margins and 0p345 for accuracy. Mean defined mathematical-expression entries are 0p281 and 0p273, compared with all-pair mathematical-expression means of 0p750 and 0p754, and on matching pairs the mathematical-expression means are 0p743 and 0p769. These overlapping intervals don't establish a size trend in the effect.

Trait removal changes the estimates more than recipe or size removal. Exclusion of BoolQ gives 1p786 with interval 1p689 to 1p877 for margins, and 1p558 with interval 1p412 to 1p687 for accuracy. Exclusion of CommonsenseQA gives the smallest point estimates, 1p096 and 1p014. BoolQ contributes 68 percent of the estimated margin variance trace and 84 percent of the accuracy trace.

The removal estimates imply that the off-diagonal terms with BoolQ are negative and sum to about mathematical-expression of the margin trace and mathematical-expression of the accuracy trace. Its removal therefore lowers the denominator by more than the numerator. The same removal lowers the aggregate standard deviation on both scales, from 0p00572 to 0p00516 on margins and from 0p00514 to 0p00326 on accuracy, as tab:original10 shows.

A larger inflation factor accompanies a less variable average. WinoGrande is the only other removal whose accuracy interval, 1p023 to 1p178, excludes one. The table gives the remaining intervals and standard deviations. We also lowered BoolQ's weight below the flat tenth in seven steps to 0p0533 and raised the other nine weights together so the battery still sums to one. Margin inflation climbs from 1p2439 at the flat weighting to 1p5030 at the smallest weight, and every interval along the way excludes one.

Accuracy climbs from 1p0784 to 1p2311 over the same steps. Its interval excludes one at a BoolQ weight of 0p0867 and below, a reduction of about one seventh. The estimate moves smoothly, so the accuracy conclusion is a knife edge in the weighting, since a battery that discounts BoolQ by a seventh reports dependence on accuracy as well. We report the flat weighting because the release gives no basis for a special BoolQ weight. The item-count weighting below is the one alternative its own counts supply. A reader with such a basis should expect the accuracy answer to move first.

Standardisation of traits by estimated seed standard deviation gives 1p803 on nine margin traits and 1p707 on eight accuracy traits. These point diagnostics omit traits with nonpositive variance estimates and divide by uncertain standard deviations, with seven accuracy traits at or below item noise. With those estimated weights held fixed, the wild recipe-cluster intervals are 1p544 to 2p023 and 1p228 to 2p076. They omit the uncertainty in the weights themselves.

We don't score them against the planning thresholds. Removal of defined traits from the standardised batteries gives margin estimates from 1p625 to 1p910 and accuracy estimates from 1p388 to 1p780. Selection of traits by positive estimated variance can change the target, and it could raise the mean correlation if the excluded low-signal traits also have low true correlations, an assumption we haven't tested.

The raw trait-removal spans are 0p690 for margins and 0p544 for accuracy, against interval widths of 0p195 and 0p164. The delete-one-trait jackknife formula gives scales of 0p554 and 0p455, against sampling standard errors of 0p045 and 0p038. Without the BoolQ removal value those scales fall to 0p139 and 0p073. These are descriptive composition diagnostics, because the ten fixed benchmarks aren't established exchangeable draws from a benchmark population.

A normal calculation on the jackknife scales and sampling standard errors added in quadrature gives ranges of 0p15 to 2p33 and 0p18 to 1p974, and both include one. Without the BoolQ value the same arithmetic gives 0p958 to 1p529 and 0p918 to 1p239. Availability of a separate competence measurement
The desired gain control requires per-run loss on held-out text separate from the benchmark items. The DataDecide fields inspected in the source analysis don't supply it. The PolyPythias evaluation files considered there cover three sizes at early and final checkpoints, yet they hold accuracies, likelihood differences, and LAMBADA perplexity on the same benchmark items, and that perplexity doesn't provide the required independent measurement scale.

The Mistral release of Karamcheti et al (2021) provides five GPT-2 Small and five GPT-2 Medium runs with about 600 checkpoints each, listed as 610 in the release summary, with WikiText-103 and OpenWebText validation perplexity. The source search didn't find the corresponding per-run benchmark battery. Without paired held-out loss and benchmark scores from the same runs, we can't use that release to identify the gain contribution.

Checkpoint noise as a proxy for seed noise
Heineman et al (2025) measure a benchmark's noise as the spread of its score over the final checkpoints of one run and compare that spread with seed variation one benchmark at a time. For an average the two measures agree only when both noise sources share one pattern of correlation across benchmarks. We compute the inflation of the equal-weight average under each from their released evaluations. Checkpoint inflation pools the covariance of each run's final five checkpoints around the run mean, and seed inflation uses the covariance across runs at the final step. Intervals come from 5,000 bootstrap draws of runs, or of recipes on DataDecide.

On the 225 DataDecide runs with five released checkpoints, checkpoint inflation of the ten-benchmark accuracy average is 1p104 [1p057, 1p156] , which lies inside our seed-based interval around 1p078. Per-byte margins give 1p574 [1p058, 1p624] , with the point estimate near its own upper limit and per-size values from 1p103 to 1p832, a sign that a few runs dominate the pooled covariance, so we don't compare margins.

The OLMo 2 1B runs of the same release carry ten initialisation seeds and eight of our benchmarks, without BoolQ and OpenBookQA. There, checkpoint inflation of accuracy per character is 0p992 [0p890, 1p087] . Seed inflation at the final step is 0p516 [0p218, 0p765] , but that step gives the lowest of the last 30 checkpoints, whose single-step seed values run from 0p516 to 1p301 with a median of 0p986. The run bootstrap at one step misses that spread.

We read the median, which agrees with the checkpoint value and with the twenty-checkpoint mean of Table 1 . Bits per byte give checkpoint inflation of 1p112 [0p995, 1p207] against a median seed value of 1p014, and nine data-order runs give medians of 0p910 on accuracy and 1p060 on bits per byte. These comparisons are exploratory, rest on ten runs of one configuration, and omit BoolQ, which carries most of our margin covariance.
