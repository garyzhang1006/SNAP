We estimate item-general covariance from two disjoint item halves per benchmark, where we centre both half scores across each configuration's replicate runs and take their cross-half products. This paper makes four contributions to the measurement of run covariance. (i) We define the item-general run covariance that cross-half products estimate, identified up to an item component shared across benchmarks and halves. (ii) The replicate standard deviation of the battery average gives the uncertainty of a rerun on the released items and is what we recommend that authors report. The split adds per-benchmark run variances net of item noise and a redrawn-bank target, untested on DataDecide, and its value parts from the replicate one when item noise is large, at 1p558 against 1p285 for accuracy without BoolQ (Section 1 ). (iii) Algorithm 1 gives the measurement recipe, with near-nominal intervals in simulation unless a run effect is shared within a model size or item noise reaches the top of our swept range, and it tells a planner that, with generic item noise, three runs per configuration resolve margin inflation while accuracy needs eight. (iv) We measure run covariance across the 375 released runs and find it concentrated within scoring formats, where every recipe, recipe-pair and benchmark deletion and four of five size-band deletions leave a margin interval that excludes one.
