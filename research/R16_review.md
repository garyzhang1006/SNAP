## Review

Summary. The paper defines an item-general cross-benchmark run covariance, estimates it with a split-half construction on 375 DataDecide runs, reports inflation factors of 1.244 (margins) and 1.078 (accuracy) with recipe-cluster intervals, and then spends most of its length on sensitivity and calibration. Both pre-registered thresholds were missed and the paper says so in the abstract.

Strengths. S1, the estimand is stated before the estimator, with the conditional independence condition made explicit and then tested on the one benchmark where it was in doubt (Section 5.1 and Appendix A). S2, inference is calibrated by simulation across twelve populations that include the failure modes a reviewer would raise (recipe-shared effects, heavy tails, one dominant cluster, correlated item errors), and the percentile alternatives are shown to fail where they fail (Table 12). S3, disclosure is complete, the unimplemented holdout, the mismatched schedules, the negative variance components, the unrun loss proxy, and the analyses this revision didn't rerun are all named in the body or the statements. S4, an independent implementation reproduces the headline numbers.

Weaknesses. W1, the contribution is a measurement on one released population with three replicates per configuration, and the accuracy interval includes one, so the paper's positive content is the margin estimate and the BoolQ composition sensitivity, which a reviewer can call thin for a main-track paper. W2, the auxiliary runs differ in training budget from the default runs below 1B, so the "run covariance" includes schedule effects, and the auxiliary contrast that removes them has wide intervals (Table 1). W3, the prediction experiment in Table 2 still lacks paired uncertainty, and the operational comparison added in this revision finds no model that beats independence, which undercuts the rank-one story a reader takes from Table 2. W4, novelty is a pairing of known constructions (Spearman split halves, the Bouthillier inflation algebra) rather than a new method, and the paper says so. W5, the writing is dense with hedges and the results sections read as a list of what can't be concluded, which costs clarity even though each hedge is warranted.

Score. Soundness 4 of 5 (the estimator and inference are right, the schedule confound limits interpretation). Presentation 3 of 5 (accurate but defensive, many "The" and "We" openers, 44 and 36 of 210 body sentences). Contribution 2 of 5 (measurement on one population, thresholds missed, no method advance). Overall 5, borderline. A reviewer who values calibrated measurement papers gives 6. A reviewer who wants a method or a positive headline gives 3, and the paper can't stop that reviewer, because the accuracy interval including one and the missed thresholds are facts, not presentation.

Can the score be made "practically impossible to be below 4"? No, and I won't claim otherwise. What this revision does is remove every reason a 3 could rest on an error, an unsupported claim, an unverifiable citation, or an undisclosed deviation. The remaining 3-risk is a judgement about contribution size, which only a second replicate population (R12, GPU) or a positive mechanism result (R7, GPU) could change.
